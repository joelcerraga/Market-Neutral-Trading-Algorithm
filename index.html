The research question

Can market structure improve a reversal signal?

A recent stock move can be compared with the moves of connected stocks. The study asks whether a graph-based version of reversal improves on a simpler baseline once both share the same constraints, execution timing and costs.

As Mantegna (1999) explains in Hierarchical structure in financial markets, correlations can describe relationships between assets. That representation motivates the analysis; it does not establish a trading advantage.

01

Construct a constrained portfolio

Target dollar and estimated SPY-beta neutrality, with gross exposure and individual position limits. Realised beta can still drift.

02

Test the network hypothesis

Compare baseline reversal with graph diffusion under the same delayed execution, trading fees and short-borrow assumptions.

03

Inspect the geometry

Use correlation surfaces and persistent homology to describe changing stock relationships, then compare descriptors with simpler controls.

Final evaluation / 2020–2025

The proposed trading advantage was not demonstrated.

Both primary strategies lost money after costs. The graph strategy's smaller observed loss does not establish a reliable improvement, and none of the four predeclared necessary evidence conditions was met.

As Politis and Romano (1994) describe in The Stationary Bootstrap, random blocks support resampling dependent observations. Here the paired, family-adjusted interval for the annualised arithmetic improvement spans −2.14 to +3.85 percentage points.

Scope of the evidence. This is a retrospective study of a fixed survivor basket using adjusted prices. It lacks a point-in-time universe, comprehensive delisting outcomes and historical stock-level borrow availability. All evaluation periods are now observed. The public repository includes saved results and synthetic tests; exact historical replay requires the separately retained input snapshots.
